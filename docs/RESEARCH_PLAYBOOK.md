# TRIPPY research playbook

## Evidence rules

Research is comparative and source-backed, not a claim of universal inventory coverage. For each external fact record: source/provider, URL or safe source identifier, checked-at timestamp, locale/currency, evidence type (`fact`, `quote`, `estimate`, `manual`, `sample`, `historic`, `unavailable`), and uncertainty. Retain source claims separately from TRIPPY’s recommendation.

Use primary/official sources for hours, closures, reservation policy, accessibility, and eligibility where practical. If a source is stale, partial, blocked, or contradictory, say so and keep the item uncertain. Do not invent a live price, opening time, award seat, current recommendation, or official MCP.

## Route and transport research

After route candidates exist, collect enough representative evidence to compare each viable family:

1. Candidate dates and flexible-date alternatives.
2. Direct flight and reasonable connection options, including overnight/long-layover burden.
3. Alternate gateway, positioning, and open-jaw options.
4. Rail, car, coach, ferry, or other regional options where plausible.
5. Door-to-door timing: airport/station transfer, check-in/security, baggage, pickup/drop-off, border, and buffer time.
6. Fare currency, cash, taxes/surcharges, points, cabin, baggage, cancellation/change terms, and checked-at time.

Cash-versus-points comparison uses a comparable cash fare, points required, unavoidable award cash cost, positioning cost, transfer ratio, transfer time/risk, and cancellation terms. Report:

`CPP = (comparable cash fare - taxes - surcharges - positioning cost) / points × 100`

State the traveler’s valuation and what the calculation excludes. An appealing CPP cannot override a hard constraint or an unacceptably risky transfer.

## City catalogue standard

Build a broad city catalogue before reducing it to the daily plan. Cover signature sights, history/museums, food/evening, neighborhoods, walks/scenery, day trips, seasonal events, and practical fallback options. Each activity should capture:

- category and city/neighborhood; duration and cost basis;
- source, checked-at time, official hours/closure/reservation evidence, and uncertainty;
- indoor/outdoor, accessibility/mobility, crowd/seasonality, and cancellation/refund notes where relevant;
- verified latitude/longitude and map confidence; and
- image source, creator/attribution where known, licensing/usage basis, and a safe fallback/no-photo state.

Never fabricate coordinates. Unmapped but valuable research remains in the catalogue and is explicitly labeled.

## Scoring and selection

Use hard constraints as filters. Score surviving activities transparently with a traveler-adjustable model such as:

`preference fit + uniqueness + evidence quality + geographic fit + schedule fit + value - transit burden - crowd risk - redundancy`

Explain the leading positive and negative factors. Maintain must/should/could tiers and a `skip/caution` list. Preserve rejected options and the reason for rejection; they may become relevant when dates, weather, price, or closures change.

## Map and photo rules

Map pins are tiered and layerable: stays, airport/rail, must, should, and could. Show a pin only when coordinate confidence supports it. Geographic lines are connections, not routing claims. Provide map controls and a list fallback; map-tile/provider failure cannot erase activities or itinerary data.

Use photos only from a source with attributable origin and a known permissible usage basis for the target artifact. If redistribution status is unknown, prefer a link/source reference or a fallback image. Do not embed private images in a public artifact.

## Itinerary feasibility pass

For every day verify fixed reservations, hours/closures, check-in/out, travel/transfers, meal/rest/luggage buffers, physical accessibility, practical capacity, and geographic order. Avoid redundant categories and multiple high-friction commitments without consent. Add weather/closure alternatives. Mark a conflict instead of quietly moving or deleting an anchor.

## Quality and stop conditions

Stop and return to the traveler when a hard constraint is missing, price/availability is too stale to decide, a source is conflicting, a visa/accessibility/cancellation condition is unresolved, an import is not approved, or an itinerary cannot be made feasible. Before locking, verify source freshness, calculation inputs, booking states, selected values, and export redaction.
